# The studio bar

What separates a page a client pays well for from a page a model produced. Read it before the direction and again
before the review. It is judgment, not a checklist: the check catches defects, this is what makes a page good.

## What a paid page has

1. **The client's own material carries it.** Their place, their product, their people, their footage, on every
   screen. A café shows its corner, its cups, its bags and its barista; a SaaS shows its own interface doing its job;
   a studio shows its work. Stock is atmosphere (light, texture, a city), never their place or product.
2. **One idea.** One sentence a stranger could repeat ("the 10's café is a corner you walk into"). Every section
   serves it; anything that does not is cut.
3. **One orchestrated moment.** One place where the page performs: the hero flies into the sign, the product turns
   in the light, the road from the farm to the cup runs sideways. Everything around it is calm and exact. Two big
   moments compete; five are noise.
4. **Type that carries the personality.** A display face with the subject's character, set large and tight, the
   headline 10-16 characters a line; a quiet text face. The type treatment is part of the design, not a delivery
   vehicle.
5. **A sober palette.** A white or near-white ground, near-black ink, one accent with life from the subject (the
   logo's copper, the shopfront's green). The photographs bring the colour, in their true colour.
6. **Motion with a job.** It explains (a process, a change), it continues (an object moving from one place to the
   next), or it answers the reader (press, open, reveal what changed). It finishes while it can be read. Nothing
   loops or scrubs only to look busy.
7. **Every part finished.** A real map with the pin on the door, a carousel that snaps and counts, a footer with every
   way to reach them, forms that send, every state, 390 px as considered as 1440.
8. **Copy with facts only this client has.** Names, streets, hours, numbers, how it is made. Plain verbs, sentence
   case, no slogans.

## What generated pages look like (calibration)

These are defaults, not choices: they appear whatever the subject. Refuse them unless the brief asks.

| Default | Instead |
|---|---|
| A cream ground with a high-contrast serif and a terracotta accent | The sober palette with the accent from the subject |
| A near-black page with one bright accent | A light page; a dark band only where the subject is night, film or music |
| A tracked uppercase eyebrow over every heading; "01 / 02" on things that are not a sequence | Headings that answer the visitor's question; numbers only for real steps |
| One word of the headline in italic or another colour | The whole headline in the display face; emphasis by size or a line break |
| Three identical cards; a big number with a small label as a section | One dominant item with its picture; a list of facts |
| A fade-and-rise on every section; a paragraph lit word by word as the "wow" | One orchestrated moment; quiet reveals only where something changes |
| An endless band of words; a button that follows the cursor | Nothing, or the client's own footage |
| A drawn map; a diagram of boxes and lines | A real map (the map recipe); the process in footage or photographs |
| A dark stage that opens on three words; a pinned section that runs past its content | Every screen holds the subject; pinned sections as long as their content |
| A text button under every animation | The kit's small corner pause icon, only on what moves for more than five seconds |
| A cropped or letterboxed hero picture | The full picture at full bleed, the focal point chosen with object-position |

## Moves by trade

Starting points, not templates. The client's material decides.

| Trade | The moment | The sections that sell |
|---|---|---|
| Café, bakery, restaurant | The hero flies into the shopfront or the counter (dive) | The road from origin to cup (journey), the product in 3D (product3d), what to take home (carousel), the bar in their footage, a real map |
| Hotel, guesthouse | The hero loop of the courtyard or the view | Rooms as a carousel with real photographs, the place in a journey, a real map, the rates in a quiet list |
| Product, shop | The product turning in the light (product3d) or its own film | Every promise pictured, the colours that change the picture, what is in the box |
| SaaS, developer tool | The interface doing its job, live, in the hero | One feature demonstrated per section, the integrations as real logos, pricing that compares |
| Studio, portfolio | The work at full size | Case studies as chapters, the people, a contact that sends |

## The review questions

Ask them of every look.mjs sheet, section by section:
- Is the subject seen? Would a stranger know what this is in five seconds?
- Is anything empty, cut, black, letterboxed or unreadable, at 1440 and at 390?
- Does the motion show something, and is it finished while it can be read?
- Is this section something only this client could have? Which default did I fall into?
- Would the client pay for this screen? If not, what would a studio change first?

## Figma (optional)

When the user wants to approve a design before code and the Figma MCP is connected: after the direction, draw the
first screen and one characteristic section at 1440 in a new Figma file (the Figma MCP's own skills say how), with
the real photographs and the chosen faces and colours. Share the link, take the feedback into the plan, then build.
Figma is for agreeing on the direction; the page is built and judged in the browser.
