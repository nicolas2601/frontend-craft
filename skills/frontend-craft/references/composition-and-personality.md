# Composition, personality and meaningful cinematic direction

Use this for an unpinned expressive surface or a result rejected as repetitive. The current brief and Noel's product-first method govern; this is not a preset layout, compulsory film or house style.

## 1. Decide the organization before styling

Write the visitor's question, the evidence that answers it and the action/change that follows. These determine the page's architecture. Do not start from a fixed list of hero, statistics, three features, alternating splits, steps, FAQ and closing CTA, then fill its slots with subject-specific nouns.

Before choosing sections, sketch a compact **claim → evidence/content → consequence** line for each one. Keep a section only when it answers a real visitor question or advances the task; surface missing proof as an assumption or unresolved content, never invent it to complete a template. Find the concept in a genuine product artifact, output, place, material, constraint, name or ritual. Try swapping in the closest competitor: if the idea still fits unchanged, it is probably category styling rather than this product's identity. This specificity test is a prompt, not a requirement that every section invent a novel motif.

Compare two small proposals with the same real-shaped content and constraints. Compare their **organizing mechanism**, not only their colors, fonts, column order or effects:

- Does the person enter through a decision, a demonstration, a spatial journey, an argument or a collection?
- What dominates first, what becomes visible next, and what can be manipulated?
- Where does evidence live: in a persistent object, contextual detail, a comparison or a separate reading section?
- How do content density, framing and the next action change as understanding grows?

These are prompts, not template categories to draw randomly. A decision-first workbench and a guided demonstration may solve a brief differently; two left-copy/right-media heroes followed by the same blocks do not become different concepts through recoloring.

Use Taste's strongest operational insight without its generator: subject-specific evidence should determine both the argument and the composition. A real live interaction is valuable when it lets a visitor test a real claim or see a consequential result; it is not a quota. Likewise, content should be complete enough to judge the design, but any sample details must be identified for confirmation and must not masquerade as proof.

Inspect the proposals with titles and brand marks mentally removed. Name the actual difference in information order, hierarchy and interaction. If the skeleton is still interchangeable, revise it before expanding. Keep a repeated pattern when it makes real tasks more predictable; familiarity and repeated components are not defects by themselves. Preserve approved structure during a narrow refinement rather than redesigning for novelty.

## 2. Make personality observable

Three mood adjectives are not a finished direction. Connect the intended feeling to something visible and specific: subject geometry, a material or illustration language, typographic voice, written phrasing, spatial relationships and interaction behavior. Choose the few mechanisms that express this product; do not decorate every category to fill a personality checklist.

A recurring motif earns its place when it transforms meaningfully or helps orientation, not merely when it repeats as background texture. A character can relate to the item being edited; a product view can reveal the consequence of a material choice; a timeline can preserve the selected event as details open. Do not paste a mascot, game-like scene or glossy object onto unrelated content because a previous example was liked.

Look for a memorable relationship rather than an unusual widget: what does this control affect, where does the result appear, and how does the form help someone understand it? Finish the representative object/component before multiplying instances. More machines, particles, cards, polygons or lines of code do not improve the fidelity of the first unfinished piece.

## 3. Frame a story instead of applying effects

For a deliberate narrative, keep a compact beat/shot plan:

`visitor question → opening evidence → obstacle/choice/change → useful revelation → resolution/next action`

Each beat needs a purpose, subject, start/end framing, and a motivated change. Decide subject placement and scale, camera height/horizon where relevant, a readable movement path and the final composition. A close-up must reveal something the wide view did not. Keep recognizable geometry, material/light and movement grammar across handoffs; vary framing and pace to clarify new information.

Stillness, a cut or an instant state change can be the right choice. Editorial rhythm is not input latency: a slow product reveal does not justify delaying a frequent control. Do not demand a camera move, fixed shots-per-scene, preloader, particles, glows, blur or constant movement merely to look cinematic.

For scroll, compare mechanisms rather than stacking all of them:

| Mechanism | Useful when | Required check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal content with a restrained entrance | The story is already legible in reading order | No hidden information or repeated effect delaying reading |
| A sticky sequence | Earlier items provide real context for later ones | It is actually a sequence; overlap preserves meaning and narrow access |
| A guided/pinned transformation | Progress through one subject explains a change | Map progress intervals to meaningful states, handoffs and a resolved end |
| Direct object exploration | The visitor needs another viewpoint or a selection consequence | User camera owns input while exploring; guide yields; reset and semantic alternatives remain clear |

Use existing platform/project mechanisms first. A guide prescribing GSAP plus Lenis is not authorization to install both or to hijack scrolling. A video seek, a live 3D scene and a static illustration have different loading, control and fallback costs.

## 4. Keep cause and consequence together

When a control promises a visible scene change, keep the relevant preview in view during the decision or provide an explicit, understandable transition to it. Updating an offscreen object while only numeric text is visible weakens the demonstration even if state propagation is technically correct.

Inspect a complete decision at the actual viewport: initial state, action, intermediate response, settled evidence and recovery/reset. Compare candidate components using the [component sourcing protocol](component-sourcing.md), not the first marketplace hit. Search quality, visual quality, semantic correctness and task usefulness are separate checks.

## 5. Choose an effect contract, not a cinematic adjective

A short request can name the desired experience; derive its implementation from the product and actual media. “Scroll-world,” “word scroll” and “Higgsfield-style” are not interchangeable:

| Requested effect | Concrete contract | Important boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Word/line reveal or reading emphasis | Paragraph progress drives a deliberate word/line emphasis or mask; text remains available and readable | Not a video/world journey; never hide necessary copy or destroy selection/reading order |
| Layered depth/parallax | A few subject-related planes move relative to each other to reveal depth or a relationship | 2.5D is not freely explorable geometry; no scroll hijack or perpetual drift by default |
| Scroll-scrubbed product film | A paused rendered clip's time/frame follows a chapter's normalized progress | Video is a sequence of views, not a rotatable object; one controller owns media time |
| Continuous connected journey | Sequential legs hand off actual decoded boundary frames and compatible camera motion | Input-image conditioning alone does not establish pixel-locked continuation |
| Dive → map → next scene | A miniature/map journey makes the pull-out and transition intentional | The same reversal can feel like rewinding in a realistic walkthrough |
| Direct product exploration | A real scene lets the person orbit/select while adjacent content explains the result | Guided camera yields to manual control; keep reset and semantic alternatives |

Choose the smallest fitting set, not every row. For an image-led direction, use a representative visual board or rendered slice to prove composition, imagery and material before expansion. Read that actual reference again while building and compare the real result; a verbal “cinematic” thesis is not the visual target. A generated board is a proposal, not approved identity, accurate copy or accessible component behavior. Do not require a paid board for every section, an entire generated asset kit or a new framework.

## 6. Camera, pacing and connected shots

Derive each shot from **what becomes understandable**. Record subject/focal point, opening and resolved framing, camera direction/height/scale, intended change, light/material continuity and outgoing motion. Distinguish physical camera travel from lens zoom; an orbit should reveal another relevant face, a lateral track a process relationship, and a rise a useful spatial context. These are possibilities, not category presets or a compulsory move in every scene.

Two source mechanisms are worth separating:

- **Continuous legs:** generate/render a leg, inspect its actual final decoded frame, then use that frame as the next leg's starting condition when the backend supports it. That dependency is sequential, not a parallel batch. An expressive orbit/crane/lateral move *inside* a leg is compatible with the concept; its exit must settle into motion that the following entry continues. The rule concerns an unintended direction/velocity flip **at the join**, not banning every non-forward movement. Exact position matching alone does not establish motion continuity.
- **Dive plus connector:** for an intentional miniature/map presentation, first obtain the independent dives. The connector's inputs are the actual previous last frame and next first frame, not their original scene stills. Its model must actually support both conditioning roles. Inspect its decoded result: even a nominal end-image input can produce a near-match rather than identical pixels. A short crossfade may soften small drift, not repair a wrong view, invented object or unintended rewind.

Select boundary frames by their actual decoded indices/timestamps. An approximate seek such as “0.15 seconds before the end” is **not proof of extracting the last frame**. Compare both sides of every join, in both scroll directions. A PSNR threshold or shared seed cannot certify visual/velocity continuity. Keep the original render and final delivery encode distinct; derive each runtime poster from the actual delivered clip's first frame so loading does not flash a different composition.

The verified Wan/LTX routes in the [video module](../modules/hf-video/SKILL.md) expose one initial image and a prompt, not exact camera-path or final-frame controls. Do not advertise a seamless multi-scene chain merely because individual clips generated successfully. First qualify one handoff. If exact continuity is essential and unsupported, use a feasible controlled renderer/owned sequence or report the media gap; choose an explicit cut or authored transition only when the brief allows it. Do not silently spend on Higgsfield/Monid to rescue it.

Plan a compact timeline in existing notes:

`chapter/question → visible proof → scroll interval → media/frame interval → copy emphasis → exit state`

Scroll distance controls reading dwell; it is not the generated clip's duration. A short film can support a longer reading interval without becoming “more generated seconds.” A purposeful **linger** holds/slows media while its key copy is read, then resumes toward the next beat. Preserve endpoints, monotonic mapping and understandable reverse behavior; do not stack so much easing/linger that the scene lags input or rushes the join. Derive chapter lengths from content, not a fixed number of viewport heights or a mandatory scene count.

## 7. Runtime contracts and actual checks

These are implementation requirements, not a claim that the uninspected upstream engine already satisfies them:

- **Progress and ownership:** compute bounded chapter progress from real layout, with finite positive intervals. Map it to a valid media/frame range, including its final decoded frame rather than a time beyond the clip. One animation/controller owns each media clock; do not fight it with autoplay, a second scroll timeline or per-frame React state. Update discrete chapter state separately. Recover correctly after resize, font loading, direct anchor navigation and reverse scroll.
- **Word/line effects:** preserve the original sentence, spaces, selection and a single coherent semantic reading. If a decorative split copy is necessary, hide only that duplicate from assistive technology and retain equivalent semantic text. Recompute line grouping after layout/font changes. Inactive words still need suitable contrast; do not use nearly invisible body text as a loading/reveal strategy. Under reduced motion show the complete legible copy without a travel/blur sequence.
- **Seekability and decode:** inspect the real host's range responses, metadata, seekable intervals, codec and actual seek behavior; neither many keyframes nor a Blob alone guarantees smoothness. Shorter GOPs trade bytes for cheaper random access. A bounded same-origin Blob can help when hosting/range behavior prevents seeking; it costs transfer/memory and needs cleanup. If used, allow `blob:` narrowly in CSP `media-src`, not by weakening the whole policy. Do not copy a universal GOP, sharpness filter, resolution or “never downscale” rule.
- **Loading and fast input:** reserve geometry and keep the matching poster until a frame is actually decoded/painted, not merely metadata-ready. Coalesce to the latest requested seek while one is pending; avoid an unbounded queue on fast flicks. A failed clip leaves meaningful static content, not a black box or endless retry. On relevant iOS targets, verify muted/inline first-gesture priming and poster handoff rather than assuming desktop behavior carries over.
- **Resource limits:** load active/nearby media under a measured byte/decoded-memory budget; do not fetch every clip or decode hundreds of full-resolution images up front. Abort stale requests, cancel RAF/listeners, detach video nodes and revoke retired Blob URLs. Changing responsive sources or mounting twice must not leave duplicate controllers. In SSR projects, render meaningful chapters/posters on the server; access browser globals only in the appropriate client lifecycle.
- **Mobile and input:** make a deliberate crop/framing decision. Test whether center-safe media preserves the story; use another composition/portrait source when it does not, without imposing a paid second chain or another questionnaire. Preserve safe areas, keyboard navigation, zoom and focus. Distinguish browser-chrome height changes from keyboard/orientation/real layout changes; do not ignore every height resize globally. Normal scrolling and a skip/anchor path remain usable.
- **Reduced motion:** for a media-heavy scrub, prefer the same complete semantic chapters over static posters, without starting video download/decode. No missing claims, CTA or part information. For an already loaded experience, changing the preference must release/stop unnecessary work and preserve location/context.
- **Acceptance:** through the host's actual browser, inspect initial poster, mid-shot, each join before/after, both directions, fast flick → settled state, loading/failure, narrow/rotated layout, keyboard and reduced motion. Check network/console and teardown. Separate emulation from actual phones and file fps from achieved rendering rate. A mechanical checklist or screenshot-safe styling does not replace looking at the real surface; a blank full-page capture needs diagnosis, not blindly removing pin spacing.
